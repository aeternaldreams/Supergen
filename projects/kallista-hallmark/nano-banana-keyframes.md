# HOLIDAY RESERVATIONS: Nano Banana prompts, cast and sets (round 1)

## How to use
- **Cast pictures: portrait (9:16 or 3:4)**, one view per image (a front close-up plus a full-body shot is ideal). Portrait keeps facial and wardrobe detail that 16:9 squeezes out. Pick clean versions with no baked-in text or labels.
- **Sets and start frames: 16:9.**
- **Step 1, cast pictures:** plain light-grey studio backdrop. These are reference pictures only; never use one as a Supergen first frame.
- **Step 2, sets:** empty locations with no people, in the shared film look.
- **Step 3, scene keyframes (next round):** attach the cast sheet(s) and the set, and compose the first frame of each Supergen clip.
- For Kallista and Adrian, attach their identity shots. Everyone else is generated from text; once you have a face you like, attach that sheet in every later prompt.
- Keep "static", "spark" and "crackle" out of every prompt.

**Camera block:** every set and start-frame prompt below already ends with the same camera line, so they all share one look. Avoid shorthand tags in prompts: Nano Banana took the old "trailer look" tag literally and drew mobile-home shells.

**Studio block:** same idea for cast pictures; each one ends with a studio camera line. Add "front head-and-shoulders close-up" or "full-body standing view" to choose the framing.

### Method: edit the set picture instead of generating a new one
Composing from references makes Nano Banana rebuild the room, so the furniture moves and the camera drifts. To lock the geometry:
1. Open the chosen set picture in Flow's edit mode ("What do you want to change?") and attach the cast portraits. The edit keeps the room's pixels and only adds people. This makes the wide master shot.
2. For closer shots, crop the master with Flow's crop tool. A crop keeps the geometry perfectly. If it looks soft, run one more edit: "Keep the composition exactly the same; add fine detail and sharpness."
3. Attach Kallista's front close-up (identity shot 1), not the coat picture. The coat picture pulls in the wrong outfit and loosens her face and hair.

### Fixing character drift: one person per edit pass
Once the room and poses look right, keep that image and fix faces one person at a time. With several people in one edit, Nano Banana splits its attention and every face drifts a little.
- **References:** use tight head-and-shoulders crops with the hair fully visible. Make an indoor Becca reference first by editing her portrait: "Remove the hat and scarf; show her full auburn wavy hair; keep her face identical."
- **Kallista pass** (attach identity shots 1 and 2): "Change only the woman with platinum-blonde hair on the sofa. Make her face and hair match the reference woman exactly: same face shape, eyes, nose and lips; platinum-blonde hair worn down, straight and sleek, center part, falling just past her shoulders. Keep her pose, sweater, wine glass, the lighting and everything else in the image exactly the same."
- **Becca pass** (attach the indoor Becca reference): the same wording for "the woman with auburn hair holding the bottle".
- **Milly pass**, if needed.
- **Cleanup pass:** "Remove the wine bottle standing on the coffee table. Change nothing else."
- Small faces in a wide shot drift most, in Nano Banana and in Supergen. Use the tighter crops for dialogue clips.
